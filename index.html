Digital Twin Design Landscape

What makes a digital twin? Not all systems described as digital twins have the same capabilities. This landscape compares digital twins and related systems in chemistry, materials science, and quantum information science across six dimensions of design, from physical–digital coupling to model fidelity, validation, and autonomy.

    Contribute to the landscape

    Submit a system so it appears for everyone. Only systems loaded from the scoring skill's JSON file can be submitted; manually entered scores cannot. A submission is listed as pending until the maintainers re-run the skill on the source paper and confirm the scores, then marked as verified.

    "Review and submit" opens a page that summarizes your system, checks that every field is complete, and prepares a GitHub issue. Include the DOI and the evidence and basis (E/I/T) for each score that the skill returns. To propose a new axis or a change to an existing one, use "Propose a modification or new axis" beside the system profile.

    Scores follow rubric v (Appendix A of Grieder and Qian, “What Makes a Digital Twin?”). Rationales, source evidence, and contribution guidelines are in the open rubric repository. Read the current rubric and decision rules.
